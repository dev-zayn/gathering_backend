# Getting listed

From a published manifest to a tile a player can open: you register it, your testers play it, a person at Gathering reviews it, and you publish it. {{ className: 'lead' }}

---

## 1. You publish the manifest

<Row>
  <Col>

    At `/.well-known/gathering-game.json` on your game's origin (or any address you give us), reachable without authentication. Nothing happens until you register the address.

    Check it from outside your own network before you send it. Two things go wrong here more than anything else, and both are invisible from inside.

    <Properties>
      <Property name="Public" type="no auth">
        We fetch it before you are listed. A login in front of it is the most common reason a registration fails.
      </Property>
      <Property name="JSON" type="body">
        A catch-all route that serves your index page instead is the second. It returns `200`, which is why it looks fine until somebody parses it: `manifest: not_json`. We parse the body; the content type is not read.
      </Property>
    </Properties>

    <Note>
      Two limits, rarely met: the document must be at most 256 KB, and we give up on a fetch
      after ten seconds. A private or localhost address is refused outright.
    </Note>

  </Col>
  <Col sticky>

    <CodeGroup title="Check it yourself" tag="GET" label="/.well-known/gathering-game.json">

    ```bash {{ title: 'cURL' }}
    curl -sS -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code} %{content_type}\n' \
      https://games.yourstudio.example/.well-known/gathering-game.json
    ```

    </CodeGroup>

    <CodeGroup title="What you want back">

    ```text
    200 application/json
    ```

    </CodeGroup>

  </Col>
</Row>

---

## 2. You register it in the console

<Row>
  <Col>

    Your studio signs in to the [Gathering console](https://console.ga3da.fun) as a **publisher**. Ask the Gathering team for an account at [ga3da.fun@gmail.com](mailto:ga3da.fun@gmail.com): they send a one-time link, and you choose your password there. The console shows your own games and nothing else.

    **My games → Register a game** takes the address. We fetch and validate the document there and then; if anything is wrong you get the whole list back at once and nothing is created. Fix it and try again.

    The game's id is the manifest's, for good. Its tier is `partner` whatever the document says, and the name on its tile is your studio's account name.

    <Note>
      You are handed a client secret, **once**. It is shown at the moment of registration
      and never again: it cannot be read back, only rolled (from the game's page), which
      breaks your server until it has the new one. Store it before you close the window.
    </Note>

  </Col>
  <Col sticky>

    <CodeGroup title="What registration returns">

    ```json
    {
      "game": {
        "id": "yourstudio.game",
        "listing": "unpublished",
        "build": { "version": "1.0.0", "status": "testing" }
      },
      "client_secret": "sec_9f2k…shown_once"
    }
    ```

    </CodeGroup>

    <CodeGroup title="Or, everything at once">

    ```json
    {
      "error": "invalid_fields",
      "fields": {
        "mode": "must be embedded",
        "room.players.max": "must be at least min and at most 64",
        "scopes": "unknown scope: orders:write"
      }
    }
    ```

    </CodeGroup>

  </Col>
</Row>

## 3. Your testers play it first

<Row>
  <Col>

    What you registered is your game's first **version**, under test. Nobody sees it yet but its testers.

    On the game's page, make a **tester's code**: eight symbols, with a label you know it by, an optional limit on how many may join with it, and 1 to 90 days. A player types it in the app under **Me → Beta games** and tests your game from then on. **Beta games** stays out of sight until it is found: tell your testers to tap the version at the bottom of **Me** five times. Once found: its tile wears "Beta", and they open, play and make rooms in the version under test.

    <Properties>
      <Property name="Test rooms" type="testers only">
        A room a tester makes plays the version under test, and only your testers can join it. A tester who joins a room of the live game plays the live game.
      </Property>
      <Property name="Testers" type="up to 500">
        Kept per game, version after version, until they leave or you take them off. Up to 20 live codes at a time.
      </Property>
      <Property name="Notes" type="text">
        What is new and what to try: testers read them on the game's page in the app, and so does the reviewer.
      </Property>
    </Properties>

  </Col>
  <Col sticky>

    <Note>
      Changed your manifest? **Read it again** on the version's card. Your page's own files
      are yours: testers load whatever your entry serves, the moment you deploy it.
    </Note>

  </Col>
</Row>

---

## 4. You send it to review

<Row>
  <Col>

    The store copy is yours to write, on the version: it goes to review with everything else, so what players read is what a person at Gathering read.

    <Properties>
      <Property name="tagline" type="string, required">
        The line under your name on the tile, in the language your players read, up to 160 characters.
      </Property>
      <Property name="icon" type="file, required">
        The tile picture, a square PNG or JPEG up to 2 MB, kept by Gathering.
      </Property>
      <Property name="description" type="string">
        Shown on your page in the store, not on the tile, up to 4,000 characters.
      </Property>
      <Property name="category" type="one of a list">
        How the store groups you, picked from the console's list. Gathering's team keeps that list, so it can grow: `speed`, `brain`, `words`, `luck`, `guess`, `draw`, `party` and `other` to start with.
      </Property>
      <Property name="screenshots" type="file[]">
        Optional, up to six, each a PNG or JPEG up to 2 MB.
      </Property>
    </Properties>

    **Send to review** freezes the version until a reviewer answers; take it back to change it. The reviewer plays it in the app with a code of their own and either **approves** it or **asks for changes**, with the reason. Asked for changes, you fix what they said and send it again. Featuring a game in the store stays Gathering's call.

  </Col>
  <Col sticky>

    <CodeGroup title="Where a version stands">

    ```text
    testing → in_review → approved → published
                   ↘ rejected (changes asked) → in_review …
    ```

    </CodeGroup>

    <Note>
      Changing an approved version's store copy, pictures or manifest takes the approval
      back: it was for what the reviewer saw.
    </Note>

  </Col>
</Row>

---

## 5. You publish it

<Row>
  <Col>

    Approved, the version waits for you. **Publish** makes it the live game: its manifest, its store copy, and, the first time, its place in the store. Every player gets it from their next open; rooms already open keep the version they started with.

    Until its first publish your game exists, has a client secret, and is a `404` to every player who is not a tester — not a `403`, because a game they cannot see should not be something they can detect.

    <Properties>
      <Property name="unpublished" type="404">
        Exists, has credentials, invisible but to its testers. Opening it is indistinguishable from opening a game that was never registered.
      </Property>
      <Property name="published" type="200">
        In the store. Players can open it, and the launch flow works.
      </Property>
      <Property name="suspended" type="404">
        Was published, is not now. Live tokens stop immediately, testers' too.
      </Property>
    </Properties>

  </Col>
  <Col sticky>

    <CodeGroup title="Opening a game you cannot see">

    ```json
    { "error": "not_found" }
    ```

    </CodeGroup>

    <Note>
      The same answer whether the game does not exist, is unpublished, or is suspended.
      Anything else would let somebody enumerate what is coming.
    </Note>

  </Col>
</Row>

## Shipping a new version

<Row>
  <Col>

    Bump the version in a manifest and start **the next version** on your game's page. It goes the same way as the first: your testers get it at once, then review, then you publish it. Players keep the live game the whole time; one version is on its way at a time.

    Your live manifest changes only through a published version: we do not re-read a publisher's game on our own.

    <Properties>
      <Property name="A test address" type="recommended">
        Players load whatever your live entry serves. Host the next version at an address of its own — say `/beta/`, with its own manifest — so testers try it without anybody else getting it.
      </Property>
      <Property name="version" type="must change">
        A next version at the live version is refused: `version: same_as_live`.
      </Property>
      <Property name="scopes, permissions, room" type="reviewed">
        The reviewer sees what your version asks for beside what the live game asks for. A wider list reaches players only once it is published.
      </Property>
    </Properties>

    <Note>
      No player is ever asked about a scope; a permission newly published, the app asks for
      as the game next opens. Tokens minted before a publish carry the old list until they
      renew.
    </Note>

  </Col>
  <Col sticky>

    <CodeGroup title="What that means in your code">

    ```js
    // A token minted before a publish carries the old
    // list until it renews. Read scope; do not assume it.
    const { scope } = await bridgeContext(token)

    if (scope.includes('identity:read')) {
      greet(player.nickname)
    } else {
      greet('يا لاعب')
    }
    ```

    </CodeGroup>

  </Col>
</Row>

## Being suspended

<Row>
  <Col>

    An admin can suspend a game at any time. It leaves every store and shelf at once and its live tokens stop with it — not at their expiry, **immediately**.

    Shelves are not emptied, so reinstating it is another publish and every tile comes back.

    What your server sees is a renewal that stops working. Treat it as removed: stop, and do not loop.

  </Col>
  <Col sticky>

    <CodeGroup title="Your next renewal">

    ```json
    { "error": "invalid_grant" }
    ```

    </CodeGroup>

    <Note>
      Indistinguishable from a player taking the game off their shelf, and handled the same
      way — see [Authentication](/auth#staying-signed-in).
    </Note>

  </Col>
</Row>
