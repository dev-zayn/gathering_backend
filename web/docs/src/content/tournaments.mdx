# Tournaments

Knockouts and leagues of your game, played in rooms you already know how to run. {{ className: 'lead' }}

Players make a tournament of your game from the app, from its store page, or from inside your
game, and their friends join it with a code or a link. An official one is made by Gathering and
listed for everybody. Your game needs nothing new to be played in one: a tournament's matches are
**match rooms**, ordinary rooms with a few rules of their own, and the results your game already
sends decide them. A game whose manifest seats two or more at a table can be played in a tournament.

<Note>
  The one thing to get right: **rank every player when a round ends**, the same way you already do
  with `room.end({ scores })` or your server's [results](/rooms#results). A tournament counts each
  round by its ranks. A round that ends with no ranks counts for nothing, and the players play it
  again.
</Note>

## The two formats

<Row>
  <Col>
    <Properties>
      <Property name="knockout" type="format">
        Duels in a bracket of 2 to 64 players (eight by default), drawn at random; when they do not
        fill it, some go through the first stage on a bye. Every match is two players; the winner
        goes on, the loser is out, and the final's winner is the champion. A match is one round, or
        the best of 3 or of 5. A round's winner is the player ranked strictly better than the
        other, or the one ranked when the other is missing from the results; equal ranks are a
        draw, which wins nobody anything, so the pair plays another round. Needs a game that seats
        two (`room.players.min` of 2 or less).
      </Property>
      <Property name="league" type="format">
        Everybody at one table, for 1 to 20 rounds (five by default). Every round gives each ranked
        player a point for every ranked player they beat; the most points after the last round wins,
        then the most first places, then the draw's order. Made for party games: up to your
        manifest's `room.players.max`.
      </Property>
    </Properties>
  </Col>
  <Col sticky>
    ```js {{ title: 'Ending a round, ranked' }}
    // Rank 1 is first; ties share a rank (1, 1, 3).
    // Leave rank out and it is computed by score,
    // highest first.
    await gathering.room.end({
      scores: [
        { player: winner, score: 1, rank: 1 },
        { player: loser, score: 0, rank: 2 },
      ],
    })
    ```
  </Col>
</Row>

## Match rooms

A match is played in a room made for it: the first of its players to press "play" makes it and
hosts it, the others join it. Your page opens in it exactly as in any room, one page load per
round. What is different:

- **Only the match's players come in**: the two of a knockout match, or every entrant still in a
  league. Anybody else typing the room's code is refused `not_in_match`.
- **No kicks, no invites**: `room.kick()` and `friends.invite()` reject `tournament_room`;
  `room.invite()` opens the app's invite sheet, where the platform refuses every invite, and
  resolves `[]` once it is closed. The host
  still starts each round, and anybody may leave.
- **The table is set by the match**: a knockout match room seats exactly two and starts only with
  both; a league's seats every entrant still in it, and starts with two of them (or your
  `room.players.min`, if more).
- **Every round counts.** Whenever a round ends with results, it is counted for the match: by the
  host's `room.end({ scores })`, by your server's results posted before the round ends, when
  the round runs past its time limit with your server's results in, or when the room closes
  during the round (its host closed it, everybody left, Gathering closed it). Results your server
  posts after the round has ended do not count.
- **The round that decides the match closes the room.** Your page hears `ended` for that round
  with `reason: "tournament"`, and the app shows the players the round's results, with the way to
  the tournament. A match decided away from the table (a walkover, an award) or a tournament
  called off closes its open room with the same reason. A match room that closes for any other
  reason (its host closed it, everybody left) keeps what was counted: its tally is the match's,
  not the room's, and the next room carries on from it.

### Knowing you are in one

`room.current()` carries `tournament`, and so does `tournament.current()`: `null` in any other
room.

```js {{ title: 'match.js' }}
const room = await gathering.room.current()
if (room?.tournament) {
  const t = room.tournament
  // { id, name, format, match, stage, stages, bestOf, need, rounds, played,
  //   tally: [{ player, nickname, wins, points }] }
  showBanner(t.format === 'knockout'
    ? `${stageName(t.stage, t.stages)} · first to ${t.need}`
    : `Round ${t.played + 1} of ${t.rounds}`)
}
```

Use it to say what is at stake: the stage (the last of `stages` is the final), the tally so far,
and, for a knockout, how many round wins take the match (`need`). Nothing about the match changes
during a round, so reading it when your page opens is enough.

Your own server sees the same block in the [bridge context](/auth): `room.tournament`, in snake
case (`best_of`), its tally lines `{ player, wins, points }`: player ids and numbers, no names.

## From inside your game

| Call | Answers |
| --- | --- |
| `tournament.current()` | the match your page's room plays, as `room.current().tournament`: `{ id, name, format, match, stage, stages, bestOf, need, rounds, played, tally }`, each tally line `{ player, nickname, wins, points }`; `null` elsewhere |
| `tournament.open()` | the app shows the tournament over your page: its bracket or its table. Rejects `not_in_tournament` outside a match room |
| `tournament.list()` | the player's tournaments of your game that are still on, and the official ones: `[{ id, code, name, format, status, entrants, size, official, yourMatchReady }]` |
| `tournament.create({ format? })` | outside a room: the app's own sheet to make a tournament of your game (`knockout` or `league` picked for the player when you say). `{ id, code }` once it is made, then the tournament takes your page's place. Rejects `in_room`, `not_a_room_game`, `invalid_fields` (a `format` that is neither), `cancelled` |
| `tournament.join({ code? })` | with a code, joins that tournament of your game (`another_game` for another game's); without one, the app's join sheet. `{ id, code }`, then the tournament takes your page's place. Rejects `in_room`, `not_a_room_game`, `invalid_code`, `cancelled`, or the platform's code (`tournament_full`, `tournament_started`…) |

A good place for "make a tournament" is next to your "play with a friend" key on your first
screen, the one that calls `room.create()`. The app's own menu on your frame offers it too, on a
page outside a room, for every game that seats two or more.

```js {{ title: 'menu.js' }}
document.querySelector('#tournament').addEventListener('click', async () => {
  try {
    await gathering.tournament.create()
    // The tournament's page takes yours: nothing more to do here.
  } catch (code) {
    if (code !== 'cancelled') gathering.showToast({ title: 'ما صارت البطولة' })
  }
})
```

## What players see

The app keeps the tournament: its code, link and QR while it takes entrants, the draw, the
bracket by stage or the league's table, each player's next match with its "play" key, and the
champion. Players hear when it starts, when their match is ready and when it ends, in the app and
on their phone. A tournament's link, `/t/{code}` beside a room's [invite link](/rooms#invite-links),
opens the app where it is installed, and elsewhere a page with its name, its game and its code
while it is open or running. You never draw a bracket; draw the match in front of you.
